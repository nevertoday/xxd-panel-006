# XXD Panel 006 · Small-Subject Paper Art-Book Cover Core

Treat the one currently selected photograph as the sole content source. Never borrow subjects, composition, palette, whitespace relationships, or copy from samples, other inputs, or historical outputs.

Generate only the resolved **transformed design frame**: below the photograph in top-bottom mode, right of it in left-right mode, or filling the complete canvas in design-only and wallpaper outputs. Never render another photo panel, seam, border, device mockup, or reserved photographic area inside this frame.

## 0. Immutable aesthetic motive

Every transformed frame must express:

**this exact photographed subject or inseparable relation → at least three identity, action, function, or emotion cues → radical reduction into one small concentrated recognisable vignette occupying about 10–20% of the design frame → the remaining 80–90% dominated by rough white, warm-white, or source-compatible pale paper → sparse fine line with slight hand instability used only to indicate essential structure → a few complete, opaque, restrained acrylic flat shapes used to establish the subject → a source-derived palette compressed to no more than four principal colours → setting implied by only the minimum line or one restrained colour plane → concise copy balanced against the vignette through art-book-cover whitespace**.

Quiet play and poetry come from radical editing, small subject scale, large paper space, and real identity cues—not from smiling faces, stars, suns, hearts, mascots, or decorative doodles. If an unrelated photo could replace the source without materially changing the vignette silhouette, key colour blocks, whitespace relationship, or copy, the result has failed.

## 1. Lock source facts first

Privately determine:

1. The one principal subject or genuinely inseparable relationship.
2. At least three source-specific cues across identity, action, function, or emotion, using contour, pose, orientation, proportion, material, colour, opening, connection, overlap, negative shape, or relational distance.
3. Which complex details can disappear and which minimum edges and colour blocks must remain for immediate recognition.
4. The vignette's intended 10–20% area in the current frame and a quiet asymmetric placement that follows source gesture and copy.
5. The colour temperature, tooth, and breathing zones of the 80–90% rough pale paper field.
6. The necessary locations of fine unstable line and the subject structure carried by each complete acrylic flat block.
7. A source-derived palette of no more than four principal colours, with one recognition or structural role for each.
8. When copy is present, its counterweight position, hierarchy, and natural line breaks relative to the tiny subject.

Never invent biography, place, event, ownership, emotion, or symbolism. Use a place, year, or number only when supplied by the user or reliably established.

## 2. Small concentrated subject, high recognition

- Reduce the principal subject or inseparable relation to one compact illustration unit occupying about 10–20% of the design frame by default. A wider or taller canvas does not justify enlarging it without cause.
- People retain pose, direction, clothing mass, and relational distance; animals retain body rhythm and head direction; plants retain growth gesture; architecture retains skyline and defining openings; objects and vehicles retain functional silhouette, negative shape, and direction; landscapes retain one source-specific terrain or spatial relation.
- Remove complex realistic detail, retaining only a few decisive edges, connections, and colour planes. The subject must remain immediately recognisable at thumbnail scale.
- Supporting shapes may only help identify the subject or minimally imply setting; they may not form a second focus.

## 3. Large whitespace is the primary spatial material

- Keep roughly 80–90% of the frame as rough white, warm white, or pale paper. Whitespace is the image's scale, quiet, and publishing character—not an empty area waiting to be filled.
- Place the vignette with restrained asymmetry. It may sit low, high, to one side, or near an edge when that follows pose, action, reading direction, and copy, but it must remain concentrated.
- Permit only a few short lines or one restrained colour plane to imply ground, horizon, enclosure, direction, or atmosphere. Never reconstruct the complete scene.
- Reject borders, columns, background patterns, scattered icons, repeated symbols, decorative grids, or random marks that consume the paper.

## 4. Fine unstable line and complete acrylic shapes

- Line only indicates structure, boundary, connection, texture, and recognition. Keep it thin, hand-drawn, and slightly unstable without turning it into nervous noise.
- A few opaque acrylic flat shapes establish the subject's main form. Blocks stay clear, complete, and restrained, with hand-painted deposits and gently irregular edges.
- Line and colour have separate jobs: line suggests structure; colour constructs the subject. Never collapse into pure line art or paint the entire paper field.
- Preserve paper grain and small human irregularities. Reject coloured pencil, crayon, wax, pastel, watercolour bloom, ink wash, heavy oil paint, realistic modelling, smooth digital illustration, and 3D.

## 5. No more than four source-derived colours

- Extract and compress the source's most identity-bearing colours, retaining no more than four principal colours beyond the paper field.
- Every colour must serve identity, material, opening, clothing, face, sign, terrain, or functional point rather than generic decoration.
- Keep blocks vivid, complete, and restrained. Prefer fewer colours whenever recognition remains strong.
- Reject rainbow palettes, transparent gradients, muddy mixtures, neon glow, corporate brand systems, uniform beige filters, and photographic lighting complexity.

## 6. Typography specific to 006

Type is **sparse book-cover composition that counterbalances the tiny vignette through large paper space**—not a headline wall, UI label, data panel, or detached advertising overlay.

### Copy

- Automatic copy derives one concise title, theme word, object name, place, or short phrase from subject identity, visible action, function, relation, known place, or the image's supported quiet meaning.
- Default to one title. Add zero to two tiny supporting elements only when they carry real information. Use places, years, or numbers only when supplied or reliably established.
- Copy must feel inseparable from the current subject. Reject generic inspiration, vague poetry, invented stories, and fake archival information added merely to resemble a publication.
- Preserve final user wording verbatim. Refine a direction or editable draft only within permission.

### Type direction

- **Role:** one sparse cover line that counterbalances the small illustration and gives the paper editorial rhythm.
- **Voice:** quiet, light, childlike without childishness, poetic, relaxed, restrained, and refined.
- **Construction:** one concise title in a simple handmade or understated book typeface, with at most two smaller supporting lines. Preserve natural proportions across writing systems.
- **Placement:** use whitespace above, below, or beside the vignette to create clear counterweight without crowding it or covering defining cues.
- **Material:** lightly imperfect dry ink, opaque print, or subtle letterpress; never crayon lettering, glow, metal, glass, or huge commercial display type.
- **Script:** preserve genuine Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, and Latin construction, punctuation, spacing, shaping, and semantic line breaks. Never impose Latin composition rules on every script.

The photographic panel contains no typography. Text-free mode contains no letters, characters, numbers, or pseudo-text anywhere in the transformed frame.

## 7. Current mode and wallpaper

The caller appends the one resolved current mode, exact dimensions, source visibility, and locked copy after this prompt. Execute only those values and never choose another mode.

- For paired output, generate only the transformed frame; the photograph is prepared and composed separately.
- Design-only and wallpaper contain no source photo, seam, or photographic placeholder.
- Recompose vignette scale, paper whitespace, and copy counterweight for every wallpaper aspect. Keep system-UI zones low-information and render no clock, icons, dock, controls, or device frame.
- A linked wallpaper always receives both the original source and the same approved anchor: the source locks identity and content; the anchor locks only subject scale, whitespace ratio, fine-line character, acrylic-block logic, four-colour restraint, paper texture, quiet tone, and typography. Never resize mechanically or chain derivatives.

## 8. Pre-generation check

1. At least three source-specific cues keep the subject immediately recognisable.
2. The vignette occupies about 10–20% of the design frame and stays concentrated and intentional, with no unjustified enlargement.
3. Rough white or pale paper occupies about 80–90%; whitespace feels balanced and quiet rather than accidentally empty or decoratively filled.
4. Fine slightly unstable line indicates only necessary structure and never becomes pure line art or noise.
5. A few complete opaque acrylic flat blocks genuinely establish the subject and retain paper tooth, painted deposits, and gently irregular edges.
6. Beyond paper, the palette has no more than four source-derived principal colours, each with an identity or structural role.
7. There is no coloured pencil, crayon, pastel, watercolour bloom, complex realism, heavy oil paint, smooth digital illustration, or 3D.
8. Copy is accurate and native to the resolved locale, counterbalancing the tiny subject through art-book-cover whitespace; text-free output has no pseudo-text.
9. Mode, size, source visibility, wallpaper safe areas, and copy obey the appended values.
10. There is no overview collage, logo, watermark, UI, mockup, second focus, or unrelated decoration.
